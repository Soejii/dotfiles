# Indonesian academic writing

## Governing principle

Write from rhetorical necessity. The structure exists because the argument requires it, not because an academic paragraph is supposed to have a fixed number of sentences or because every section needs matching subsections.

Indonesian academic prose should make reasoning traceable. The Universitas Negeri Malang writing guide describes scientific Indonesian as logical, direct, clear, economical, formal, and centered on ideas. Treat those as decision criteria, not decorative adjectives.

Primary guidance used for this reference:

- Universitas Negeri Malang, *Pedoman Penulisan Karya Ilmiah*, 6th edition: https://fik.um.ac.id/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Pedoman-Penulisan-Karya-Ilmiah.pdf
- Official EYD V portal: https://ejaan.kemdikbud.go.id/
- Hermen Gusri's Indonesian PJP introduction study: https://ejournal.unib.ac.id/index.php/jwacana/article/view/6669

Institutional rules are style-specific evidence, not universal law. The assignment's instructions and relevant institution outrank these references.

## Think like a writer

Before choosing sentences, establish:

1. What does the reader need to understand here?
2. What claim or question moves the document forward?
3. What evidence permits that move?
4. What interpretation belongs to the writer rather than the source?
5. What uncertainty, boundary, or competing explanation matters?
6. What should become possible in the next paragraph because this paragraph exists?

Academic depth comes from developed reasoning, not accumulated pages. Expand a section by adding necessary context, distinctions, evidence, comparison, qualification, or interpretation. Stop when its rhetorical work is complete. Remove repetition that only restates the same proposition.

## Build an argument

Distinguish these roles:

- **Claim:** the proposition the writer asks the reader to accept.
- **Evidence:** source material that bears on the claim.
- **Interpretation:** the reasoning that explains how the evidence supports, limits, or complicates the claim.
- **Qualification:** the conditions under which the claim remains defensible.
- **Counterposition:** a credible alternative that changes the argument if ignored.
- **Implication:** what follows for the document's problem or purpose.

A paragraph does not need every role. The section as a whole must make the important links explicit. Do not place evidence after a claim and assume the relationship explains itself.

Use calibrated verbs. `Menunjukkan`, `berkaitan`, `memprediksi`, `menjelaskan`, and `menyebabkan` express different evidentiary force. Match the verb to the design and result of the inspected source.

## Organize by rhetorical function

Use headings only when the reader benefits from a genuine change of topic, level, method, or argumentative function. Do not add a heading for every small idea. Do not force parallel heading structures when the material is not parallel.

Introductions vary by assignment. Possible functions include:

- orienting the reader to the problem;
- defining a contested or necessary concept;
- establishing the practical or theoretical situation;
- synthesizing what relevant research establishes;
- identifying a limitation, tension, inconsistency, or unanswered question;
- justifying why the present discussion is needed;
- narrowing the scope;
- stating the purpose, question, or position.

These are possible moves, not a mandatory checklist. Indonesian rhetorical research using the Problem Justifying Project model found variation in which moves appeared. The UM guide likewise distinguishes a makalah introduction from a research-article introduction. Determine the required work from the actual assignment and researched convention.

Do not make page count the completion rule. A longer introduction is justified only when it performs more necessary work. As one institutional example, UM limits a research article introduction to 20 percent of the text, while its makalah guidance defines completion through background, bounded topic, and purpose. Use such ratios only when the controlling institution adopts them.

## Develop paragraphs

Give each paragraph one controlling purpose. It may introduce, support, compare, qualify, interpret, transition, or conclude. A paragraph becomes incoherent when it changes purpose without preparing the reader.

Build internal movement rather than a fixed template:

1. Establish the point or situation when the reader needs orientation.
2. Develop it with evidence, explanation, contrast, or an example.
3. Interpret the material instead of merely repeating it.
4. End where the argument naturally hands off, not with an automatic summary sentence.

Topic sentences often help, especially in long analytical prose, but their position and form depend on the paragraph's job. A bridge paragraph may be shorter than an analytical paragraph. Unequal paragraph lengths are normal when their work differs.

Make relationships explicit and accurate. Use transitions because the logic requires addition, contrast, cause, condition, consequence, concession, sequence, or synthesis. Do not rotate transition words as decoration. Resolve vague references such as `hal ini` when several earlier ideas could be the referent.

## Write effective Indonesian sentences

Prioritize a visible subject-predicate relationship and keep modifiers close to what they modify. Break a long sentence when its logical relations become difficult to recover. Combine short sentences only when the combined relationship becomes clearer.

During revision, check for:

- missing or competing subjects;
- stacked clauses without a clear hierarchy;
- `di mana` used as a general relative connector instead of a place expression;
- incorrect separation of the prefix `di-` and the preposition `di`;
- redundant pairs such as `adalah merupakan`;
- repeated plural marking when one marker is sufficient;
- foreign terms used when an established Indonesian term is clearer;
- unexplained technical terms;
- excessive passive constructions that hide the actor when the actor matters;
- first-person references that displace attention from the object of discussion;
- conversational appeals such as `seperti kita ketahui bersama`;
- words or sentences that add length but no information.

Use KBBI for standard forms, an authoritative disciplinary glossary for technical terms, and EYD V for spelling and punctuation. Italicize an unabsorbed foreign term when the controlling style requires it. Maintain one term for one concept unless a distinction is intentional.

Never use an em dash. Use commas, semicolons, colons, parentheses, or separate sentences according to the actual relation.

## Preserve a natural academic texture

Do not imitate Suji's previous writing or a group member's prose. Naturalness comes from decisions that respond to the argument, not from planted errors, slang, random sentence-length variation, or informal fillers.

Prefer:

- specific claims over polished generalities;
- evidence-driven asymmetry over matching paragraph shapes;
- interpretation that takes a defensible position;
- transitions that name the real relationship;
- selective explanation based on reader need;
- meaningful variation caused by different paragraph functions.

## Formulaic-prose audit

Identify exact passages exhibiting these patterns and revise the underlying reasoning:

### Symmetrical paragraph machinery

Several paragraphs repeat the same sequence, such as definition, citation, explanation, implication. Reconsider each paragraph's actual function. Do not merely shuffle sentences.

### Empty signposting

Phrases announce importance, complexity, or relevance without stating what is important, complex, or relevant. Replace the announcement with the substantive relationship.

### Rephrased repetition

Successive sentences restate one proposition with synonyms. Keep the strongest formulation and use the space for evidence, qualification, or interpretation.

### Automatic closure

Every paragraph ends with `Dengan demikian`, `Oleh karena itu`, or a miniature conclusion. Retain closure only where the reasoning reaches a consequence that the reader needs.

### Forced grouping

Ideas appear in neat groups of three without a conceptual reason. Use the number of distinctions the subject actually contains.

### Generic academic filler

Sentences such as `Fenomena ini menjadi isu penting di era modern` add no bounded claim. State who is affected, what changed, why it matters, and what evidence establishes it.

### Unsupported confidence

The prose sounds certain because it omits limitations. Restore the conditions and strength supported by the source.

### Excessive structural polish

Too many headings, previews, recaps, and parallel subsections make the organization more visible than the argument. Remove navigation that does not help the reader.

The audit must return locations, diagnoses, and revision reasons. Never report an AI probability or rewrite text merely to lower a detector score.

## Group boundaries

Read adjacent group sections for continuity, not as a style template. Within Suji's authorized section, the writer may reorder material, introduce terminology earlier, adjust the final sentence, or add a necessary bridge paragraph. If coherence requires editing another person's section, explain why and wait for authorization.

State a concern when voice, terminology, evidence quality, or formatting differs visibly across the assembled document. Do not lower Suji's section quality to conceal that difference.
